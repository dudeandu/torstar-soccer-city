Thousands of kilometres of sewage and stormwater collection pipes run below the city of Toronto, with sanitary sewers collecting water from household drains and toilets and storm sewers collecting rainwater and snowmelt.

In older parts of the city sewage and stormwater flow into combined sewers – some a century old – where pressure from intense rainfall can build until the excess explodes into basements or streets.

On Aug. 7, 2018, city water gauges recorded up to 75 mm of rainfall in downtown areas that have combined sewers. Many of these areas were among those hardest hit by flooding.
