The fan: Personal injury lawyer Joseph Zayouna, 49

Cheering for: Iraq, the last nation to enter the World Cup only made one appearance in 1986, ending a 40-year drought. Iraq is a country of 46 million in West Asia located in the Middle East; 10,732 people of Iraqi origin live in Toronto.

Why he's cheering: Growing up in Iraq during their first World Cup gave Joseph Zayouna his love for soccer.

He recalls the times he spent crowding around a TV in 1986 at social clubs or a friend's house just to catch a game, moments that brought everyone in the neighbourhood together.

“I remember all the celebrations, the music, people on the streets, the flags. It was a great time, even though I was only really young,” said Zayouna.

Even coming to Canada, soccer was still a big part of his life. Zayouna used to play in high school and helped organize recreational games with the Iraqi community in Thornhill. Friends and family of different ages and skills would come together to share the game.

“It's just so much fun for all of us to come together and play,” said Zayouna. 

While he doesn't hit the pitch as much as he used to, he’s glad to see the next generation continue the sport and still host games in the neighbourhood.

Over the years the Iraqi community has grown in Toronto with gatherings being a big part of what continues their soccer traditions. For Zayouna as he couldn’t be prouder of his country for making it back to the World Cup and the moments leading up to the games feel “surreal.”

“Canada is home now, but Iraq's always my home as well,” said Zayouna. “For Iraq, your home country to make it, it's a big deal for us.”

The most important part is finding a place to catch the game. 

“We usually go somewhere, we choose a place to watch, either a friend's house, a restaurant or somewhere that we can watch the games and it brings everybody together,” said Zayouna. “It's that unity that soccer brings, you can't compare it to anything else.”
