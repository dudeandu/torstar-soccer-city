The fan: Partnership manager Antoine Marion, 29

Cheering for: Belgium, a country with a long World Cup history. They’ve had 15 appearances and finished third in 2018. Belgium has a population of 11.9 million people and is located in Western Europe; 530 people of Belgium origin live in Toronto.

Why he's cheering: Antoine Marion’s lucky scarf stayed with him as he followed the Belgium national soccer team.

He has many memories of following soccer at an early age and catching a glimpse of his favourite team at King Baudouin Stadium in Belgium.

“Just the atmosphere that we had on the day of the game with all the Belgian fans, a sunny beautiful day, I just like chanting all together, the whole day cheering the team,” said Marion. “The connection that you have with both friends and just random people, that's a nice thing.”

Each game brought new adventures with his group of six friends, soaking it all in as Marion’s scarf blew in the wind.

Then one day he lost it.

“To be totally honest, I think I wasn't that sober that evening and it didn't help to keep the scarf,” said Marion, who has since gotten a new one. “Even without that old scarf I'm sure I'll find another lucky charm for us to go for at this World Cup.”

Now living in Toronto for the past two years, Marion is still able to hang out with the same group of friends to cheer on his home country. They even have plans to catch the Belgium game in Vancouver against New Zealand on June 26, plans he says took six years in the making.

“It has been years that we've been waiting for this and now a couple of months ago when seeing that Belgium was playing in Vancouver we organized everything to make sure that we would be there,” said Marion.

Despite not living in Belgium anymore Marion still feels a deep connection to his home country, and is excited to celebrate the World Cup with other Belgians in Toronto.

“I'm not in the country but on the other hand there is a strong community here that helps to feel a bit like home,” said Marion.
